How the index works

Methodology

The Open Learning Index publishes a deliberately selective set of free learning resources. Candidates are screened, reviewed with evidence, compared with realistic alternatives and then re-verified on a risk-based schedule.

Two scores, two questions

Quality Score

How good is this learning resource on its own terms?

The weighted score measures pedagogy, depth, practice, materials, currency, provider expertise and accessibility.

Recommendation Score

How strongly should a learner choose this over realistic alternatives today?

This is editorial judgement and may account for prerequisites, time-to-value, friction, duplicated coverage, current availability and stronger alternatives.

Quality componentWeight

Currency is subject-sensitive. A stable mathematics lecture is not penalised merely for age; fast-moving AI, software, security, finance and legal resources are held to a much stricter currentness bar.

Quality tiers

S+9.50–10.00Reference-level, exceptional
S9.00–9.49Excellent
A+8.50–8.99Strongly recommended
A8.00–8.49Very good

Resources below A are normally excluded from the published core index.

What “free” means

TierLearner accessPublished core
F0Complete course/path + free provider completion credentialYes
F1Complete learning path + meaningful free assessment/labs, no free formal credentialYes
F2Substantial complete teaching content, without a free formal completion pathYes
F3Preview, trial, first module only or materially incomplete free accessNo

Certificates and academic credit are tracked separately. A badge is not academic credit, and neither automatically increases pedagogical quality.

Evidence and admission

Official course and provider documentation is the preferred evidence for access, pricing, credits, languages, course status and syllabus claims. Independent reviews and community discussion can supplement learner-experience evidence, but they are not accepted as the sole proof of pricing, credit or access.

Institutional prestige alone is not enough. A course is published only when it is strong enough and either clearly outperforms relevant alternatives or adds a materially different learning path.

Continuous maintenance

Published courses are re-verified according to risk and subject volatility:

  • 60–90 days for fast-moving areas such as AI, current software, cybersecurity and cloud training.
  • 120–180 days for moderately changing professional fields.
  • 365 days for stable foundational subjects and maintained archival teaching.

Pricing changes, broken links, course retirement, major edition changes, credential changes, quality regressions or a credible new challenger can trigger an immediate review.

Editorial independence and auditability

The contribution rules prohibit affiliate, referral and tracking links. Provider marketing copy is not copied into editorial fields, and provider prestige does not determine admission.

The repository is the source of truth. Canonical data, screening decisions, Deep Reviews, admission decisions and maintenance records are versioned publicly so published claims can be audited.
