## History & Culture

Seven candidates advance, three are held and one is rejected.

Yale's Civil War, Early Middle Ages and American Revolution courses advance because complete university-level sequences still provide distinctive depth in stable historical subjects. OpenLearn's 2021 suffrage course advances for source-analysis practice and free completion recognition; its 2024 Herodotus course advances because it turns ancient historiography into a current exercise in evaluating evidence and competing accounts. OpenLearn's *The Enlightenment* and Saylor's 39-hour *Modern Revolutions* also advance.

Early Modern England is held for portfolio-level archive redundancy, not for a quality defect. *France Since 1871* is held because a 2007 treatment of relatively modern history deserves a historiography/bibliography currency check. FUN MOOC's French-original music-history course is held because the official page still describes progress only until a course-closing date without exposing a clearly current session window.

Yale's *European Civilization, 1648–1945* is rejected from the finalist pool. It remains educational, but a broad Western archive survey is directly dominated for this index by broader and more current world/global-history survivors and would deepen an already documented Western-canon concentration.

## Humanities & Philosophy

Eight candidates advance, one is held and two are rejected.

Yale's *Foundations of Modern Social Theory*, *Modern Poetry*, *Dante in Translation*, both academic biblical-studies courses and *Introduction to Theory of Literature* advance because their core subject matter is comparatively stable and the public teaching sequences are deep enough to merit direct comparison. Saylor PHIL103 and PHIL203 advance as long-form, assessed, free-certificate alternatives to lecture archives.

OpenLearn *Human rights and law* is held. The course is substantial and freely completable, but it was last updated in 2019 and explicitly teaches UK legal material around the Human Rights Act; legal currency and the candidate's temporary humanities categorisation must be resolved first.

Yale *Milton* is rejected because the public archive omits several numbered lectures and is therefore a weaker specialist package than complete literature alternatives. MIT *Classics of Western Philosophy* is also rejected: the notes and assignments are useful, but it lacks a full teaching-media layer and materially overlaps stronger already-advanced MIT philosophy candidates while adding further Western-canon concentration.

## Project, Product & Leadership

Four candidates advance, three are held and three are rejected.

HP LIFE *Effective Leadership* advances: the course launched in 2024, remains actively listed, and has a current free Certificate of Completion path. Saylor BUS402 advances with 52 hours of structured project-management study, current PMI framing, free certification and college-credit recommendation. OpenClassrooms' French project-management course and English *Manage a Team* both advance because they were updated in 2025 and provide current, assessment-backed practical coverage.

OpenLearn's 35-hour project-management course is held despite its depth because the last update was in 2016. Saylor *Leadership and Teams* and *Elements of Ethical Leadership* are held for head-to-head redundancy checks against stronger current leadership survivors.

OpenLearn *Step up to leadership* is rejected because its four-hour, 2020 material is explicitly policing-centred and is dominated by broader current alternatives. *Managing and managing people* is rejected because its 2019 eight-hour treatment is now directly dominated by newer practical management courses. Saylor *Decision-Making* is rejected because four hours of basic decision-process instruction is too narrow for a crowded finalist pool.

## Methodological lessons

1. **Archive age is not an automatic rejection rule.** Stable historical, literary and philosophical foundations can survive when the public learning package is complete and distinctive.
2. **Redundancy must operate at portfolio level.** A strong Yale or MIT course can still be rejected when the pool already contains a stronger or broader route serving the same role.
3. **Modern history needs more currency scrutiny than ancient or medieval history.** Historiography, framing and bibliography can move even when the underlying events do not.
4. **Management and project courses need a real freshness bar.** A deep 2016 course can be held when professional frameworks and workplace practice have materially evolved.
5. **Short courses survive only when they add something scarce.** Current French-language project management and modern historical-method practice clear the bar; generic four-hour management primers do not.
6. **A live page is not enough for time-bounded MOOCs.** Current enrolment/session mechanics must be explicit before advancement.
