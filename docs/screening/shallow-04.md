### Reject

- Language Transfer Intro to French — strong teaching method, but intentionally introductory and outcompeted by complete free French pathways.
- Language Transfer Complete German — rejected mechanically because the provider explicitly states the course remains unfinished; F3 cannot enter the main ranking.

### Signal

Language Transfer demonstrates why the index separates pedagogy from credentials: its complete courses can advance at F2 because the reasoning-led teaching method is unusually distinctive. Minato advances for the opposite reason: a long, structured learner journey with progress tracking, assignments and tests. TV5MONDE and Deutsche Welle add large public-service exercise ecosystems and authentic audiovisual material.

The OpenLearn metacognitive course is retained because it teaches how to learn languages rather than duplicating a target-language pathway. This is a materially different learning outcome.

## Cross-tranche lessons

1. **Practice beats polish.** Interactive creation, assignments and learner output are stronger signals than attractive instructional pages alone.
2. **A certificate does not turn onboarding into a top course.** Canva essentials is the clearest example.
3. **Completeness is non-negotiable.** Language Transfer German is rejected even though the available lessons are good.
4. **Reference resources and courses should remain distinct.** musictheory.net may deserve a future companion resource index, but not a finalist slot under the current course/open-learning definition.
5. **Age is contextual.** Stable music theory or art history can survive older publication dates; obsolete software dependencies and broken learner paths cannot.
6. **Provider diversity is useful only when the learning model adds value.** Public-service language platforms, reasoning-led audio, university courses and interactive production tools can all coexist because they teach differently.
