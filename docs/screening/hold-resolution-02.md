# Hold Resolution 02 — 2026-09-12

## Scope

This pass resolves the **34 current holds** that remained after Hold Resolution 01. It deliberately separates three questions that were previously entangled:

1. is the learner route currently accessible and complete enough;
2. is the subject matter current enough for the field;
3. even if valid, does the course add enough marginal value to a selective finalist pool.

A course can therefore be current, free and credential-bearing and still be rejected for redundancy, limited depth or vendor concentration.

## Methodological lessons

1. **Currentness is field-dependent.** A 2022 deep-learning course may still deserve comparison if its pedagogy and tooling remain unusually strong; a 2020 legal or financial course can fail because changing rules are central to the learning outcomes.
2. **Depth and uniqueness are separate gates.** A short course can advance when it owns a narrow, current specialist role; a longer course can still be redundant.
3. **Provider concentration matters before deep review.** The index should not spend scoring effort on several near-overlapping vendor paths merely because each is individually valid.
4. **Jurisdiction-specific does not automatically mean reject.** Saylor BUS205 advances because its US scope is explicit and recently maintained; jurisdiction becomes a recommendation/accessibility factor later.
5. **Uncertainty remains explicit.** Two holds are carried forward because the missing evidence is material and cannot be responsibly inferred.
