# Shallow Screening 03 — 2026-09-10

## Scope

This tranche screens **30 candidates** across the three most currency-sensitive areas in the current review sequence:

- 10 Psychology & Behaviour
- 10 Health & Medicine
- 10 Education & Teaching

The gate is intentionally stricter than in earlier tranches. In psychology and health, provider reputation or a free certificate cannot compensate for dated science, shallow scope or a clearly stronger current alternative. ## Psychology & Behaviour

### Advance

- Saylor PSYCH101: Introduction to Psychology
- MIT 9.00SC: Introduction to Psychology
- OpenLearn: Forensic psychology
- OpenLearn: Psychology around the world
- NAU: Saúde Mental e Bem-Estar no Ensino Superior

### Reject

- MIT 9.00 (2004): superseded by the later 9.00SC independent-study edition and carries unnecessary scientific-currency risk.
- OpenLearn Making sense of ourselves: useful sampler, but redundant against deeper candidates.
- OpenLearn Investigating psychology: too short and narrow for the finalist pool.
- OpenLearn Starting with psychology: introductory sampler rather than a competitive foundation.
- OpenLearn Understanding depression and anxiety: too old for a mental-health evidence domain where terminology, causal models and clinical framing evolve materially.

### Signal

The MIT decision is deliberate: **brand strength never overrides a better version of the same learning path**. The later 9.00SC survives because it is purpose-built for independent study and exposes a more complete learner package; the 2004 version does not earn a duplicate deep-review slot.

## Health & Medicine

### Advance

- TGHN ICH Good Clinical Practice E6(R3)
- TGHN Research Ethics Online Training V2
- TGHN The Data Life Cycle: Practices and Policies
- WHO Good Practices for Clinical Trial Design and Implementation
- WHO Chemical Hazards Part I
- NAU Saúde das Populações para um Futuro Sustentável
- FAO Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods: Basic concepts

### Hold

- WHO One Health: Basics of multisectoral collaboration — authoritative and current, but the short scope and exact current credential route need direct value verification.

### Reject

- TGHN Introduction to Data Management for Clinical Research Studies: a useful short module, but directly outcompeted by the broader updated Data Life Cycle pathway.
- TGHN Introduction to Clinical Research: multilingual and certified, but roughly an orientation-length experience rather than a world-class course.

### Signal

A short professional module can be legitimate, useful and certified and still fail this index. Conversely, a WHO course can advance at F2 when its **content currency and authority are exceptional**, even while credential mechanics remain unresolved. Free-credential status and course quality are separate dimensions.

The NAU population-health course is a particularly strong discovery because it combines Portuguese-European instruction, substantial workload, assessed completion, a free certificate and **2 ECTS**.

## Cross-tranche lessons

1. **F0 is not a quality guarantee.** Free certification does not rescue a shallow, redundant or dated learning experience.
2. **Currency thresholds are category-specific.** A 2011 foundational psychology course can survive when unusually complete; an older mental-health treatment may not.
3. **Redundancy is a real rejection reason.** The index is not a catalogue of every good course from a strong provider.
4. **Availability is part of access quality.** Cohort/course-window material can be held even when currently free and excellent.
5. **Language diversity can add real value, but not immunity.** pt-PT courses still have to pass the same quality gate; the strong NAU records advance because of substance, not because of language alone.
6. **Credential claims remain independent from access claims.** A course may advance while its certificate mechanics stay unresolved, provided the freely available educational experience itself is strong enough.
