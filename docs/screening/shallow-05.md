## Natural Sciences

Eight of nine candidates advance. MIT Classical Mechanics, Principles of Chemical Science and Introductory Biology remain competitive because they expose coherent teaching and assessment packages rather than isolated lectures. Saylor BIO101 and CHEM101 advance because they add structured assessment and free certificates; Khan AP/College Biology and Chemistry add mastery-based practice; NASA Open Science 101 fills a distinct scientific-practice role.

Yale Principles of Evolution, Ecology and Behavior is held rather than rejected. Its academic quality and completeness are clear, but an archive-era biology/ecology course deserves a specific scientific-currency review before deep scoring.

## Engineering & Electronics

MIT 6.01SC, Nordic nRF Connect SDK Fundamentals and Nordic Bluetooth Low Energy Fundamentals advance. They provide unusually substantial practical work.

Three Nordic courses remain on hold: Cellular IoT Fundamentals, Wi-Fi Fundamentals and Matter Fundamentals. The course content itself may be free and complete, but the project must verify whether a learner can obtain the intended practical experience without buying specific development hardware, connectivity or ecosystem components. Hardware cost is not mislabeled as a course fee; it is recorded as a practical-access dependency.

## Mathematics & Statistics

All five screened candidates advance: Harvard Stat 110, MIT 18.650, MIT 18.05, MIT Real Analysis and MathWorks Optimization Onramp.

This category demonstrates why the index does not require certificates. Harvard and MIT can qualify at F1/F2 through exceptionally complete free teaching and assessment resources, while MathWorks contributes a different model: browser-native practice, automated feedback and a free certificate.

## Methodological lessons

1. **Hardware cost and course cost are different facts.** A free embedded course may still receive an accessibility penalty if meaningful labs require a development board.
2. **Scientific currency is domain-specific.** Archive age is a stronger risk in biology/ecology than in classical mechanics or real analysis.
3. **Interactive assessment matters.** A certificate is useful, but formative feedback and problem checking can be more educationally valuable.
4. **Vendor dependence is not automatic rejection.** It becomes a transferability/accessibility factor in deep scoring.
5. **A zero-rejection tranche is acceptable when the source pool is unusually strong.** Selectivity is measured across the full screening programme, not forced artificially within every batch.
