## History & Culture

All eight selected candidates advance, but for different reasons. Recent OpenLearn material contributes methodological and post-imperial perspectives; Saylor adds long-form assessed pathways; Khan Academy adds mastery-oriented practice; the French-original FUN Egyptology course improves linguistic and subject diversity; and the strongest Yale archives remain sufficiently complete to justify deep comparison.

Archive age is recorded rather than treated as an automatic failure. Deep review must still penalise canonical/geographic concentration where several courses compete for the same final-list need.

## Humanities & Philosophy

Seven candidates advance and one is held. Yale Political Philosophy, MIT Problems of Philosophy and Ethics, Saylor Political Science/Critical Thinking, OpenLearn What is politics?, and the French-original FUN political-philosophy course all clear the completeness threshold.

Yale Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature is held because its distinctive value depends partly on cognitive-science evidence described as contemporary when the course ran. The philosophy remains useful, but the empirical component needs a scientific-currency check before scoring.

## Finance & Economics

Five candidates advance, two are held and one is rejected.

MRU Principles of Microeconomics, Saylor ECON101, MIT 14.03 and MIT 14.454 advance because they provide substantial, assessable or unusually current economics pathways. Khan Financial Literacy advances with an explicit jurisdiction flag.

Yale Capitalism is held because a course framed around the 2008 crisis now faces much newer direct alternatives. MSE's Academy of Money is held because UK tax, borrowing, mortgage, investment and pension guidance was last updated in 2022.

MRU Money Skills is rejected from the finalist pool: seven lessons across a few narrow topics do not add enough depth beside stronger personal-finance candidates.

## Law & Public Policy

Three candidates advance, four are held and one is rejected.

WIPO DL-101 advances as a current, free, assessed and certificate-bearing international IP course. Council of Europe HELP Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights advances after resolving a direct learner enrolment route and confirming its 2025 update, interactive tests and electronic Statement of Accomplishment. NAU Direitos e deveres no digital advances at F1 rather than inheriting an unverified F0 claim; the current public page supports mandatory assessment but does not explicitly establish a free completion credential.

Saylor Business Law, OpenLearn Scottish Parliament and law making, the HELP Introduction to the ECHR and ECtHR, and NAU Consumidor Ciberseguro are held for jurisdiction/currency verification. The ECHR course is a particularly important catch: the currently surfaced course brief still says the Council of Europe has 47 member states, so a current learner-facing version must be verified before advancement.

The generic HELP Self-Learning Courses record is rejected because it is an umbrella catalogue, not one comparable course, and the candidate universe already contains specific HELP courses.

## Methodological lessons

1. **Archive age is subject-sensitive.** Historical and philosophical archives can remain strong while empirical, financial and legal claims demand stricter currency checks.
2. **A free certificate cannot rescue weak scope.** MRU Money Skills is valid and free but too narrow relative to stronger alternatives.
3. **Umbrella catalogues should not compete with courses.** Provider catalogues distort head-to-head ranking and duplicate their individual courses.
4. **Discovery access claims can be downgraded.** NAU Direitos e deveres no digital moves from provisional F0 to F1 because current evidence proves assessment, not a free formal credential.
5. **Current legal facts need direct verification.** A stale factual claim is enough to block advancement even when the provider is authoritative.
6. **Original-language diversity matters only when quality clears the same bar.** French and pt-PT candidates advance because they add credible learning value, not merely language coverage.
