# Shallow Screening 02 — 2026-09-10

## Scope

This tranche deliberately moves away from the high-confidence calibration bias of Shallow 01. It screens **27 mixed candidates** across Marketing & Sales, Finance & Economics, Business & Entrepreneurship, Project/Product/Leadership and Writing & Communication, with explicit head-to-head redundancy checks.

The objective remains binary workflow triage rather than final scoring: does the candidate deserve a deep review, need a specific evidence resolution, or no longer justify further review time?

## Advances

- HubSpot: Inbound Sales, Social Media Marketing, Email Marketing, Revenue Operations, Digital Advertising and AEO Fundamentals.
- Saylor: BUS203 Principles of Marketing, BUS103 Introduction to Financial Accounting, BUS300 Operations Management, BUS305 Foundations of Entrepreneurship and PRDV002 Professional Writing.

The common reasons for advancing are either unusually strong free assessment/credential value, enough instructional depth to survive comparison, current specialist relevance, or materially different learning outcomes from nearby candidates.

## Holds

### HubSpot — Inbound Marketing Optimization

The course is currently free and complete, but the index already contains several stronger HubSpot specialist pathways. It remains on hold until deep-comparison work establishes whether its optimisation focus is sufficiently unique to justify another HubSpot finalist.

### OpenLearn — Companies and financial accounting

The course is genuinely free and complete, but it is only six hours and includes company-law/reporting context that is jurisdiction-sensitive. Keep only if its specific learning outcomes are not already better served by a stronger accounting pathway.

### MIT — Leadership Development

Strong assignments and self-assessment exist, but the public teaching package is less complete than leading current alternatives. Hold pending a direct completeness comparison.

### MIT — Communication for Managers

The public OCW package contains useful readings and assignments, but relatively little direct teaching material. Hold pending comparison with the more complete MIT Management Communication course and current OpenLearn/Saylor alternatives.

## Rejects

### HubSpot — Inbound Certification

Rejected for **provider-path redundancy**, not because it is poor or paid. Its broad overview is already covered more effectively by the specialist HubSpot candidates that advance.

### OpenLearn — Marketing in the 21st Century

Still free with a Statement of Participation, but too dated for a fast-changing marketing field when multiple current alternatives exist.

### OpenLearn — First steps in innovation and entrepreneurship

Useful introductory material, but insufficient depth to beat the stronger Saylor and MIT entrepreneurship candidates in a deliberately selective final index.

### OpenLearn — Understanding operations management

Still usable and free, but only four hours, last updated in 2019 and materially shallower than the stronger full-length operations candidate.

### MIT — Introduction to Financial and Managerial Accounting (2004)

Structurally complete, but the 2004 publication date creates avoidable financial-reporting and accounting-standards currency risk when newer free complete alternatives are available.

## Evidence notes

- HubSpot Academy currently describes its Academy library as free and online, and the screened certification pages explicitly label the relevant paths as free certification courses.
- Saylor University currently states that its skill-development courses and course-completion certificates are free; certificates are earned through a Certificate Final Exam.
- OpenLearn course pages explicitly expose free-course status and free Statements of Participation, with badges where applicable.
- MIT OpenCourseWare remains F2 for these records: substantive open learning materials are free, but there is no MIT OCW completion credential.

## Important methodological signal

This tranche produces the first real rejections. That is healthy. A course can be legitimate, free and well produced and still fail this index because a stronger or more current alternative makes it redundant.

Provider reputation is not an admission criterion. MIT, OpenLearn and HubSpot candidates were all rejected or held where the learning package did not justify another finalist slot.
