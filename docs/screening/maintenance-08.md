## MIT 6.7220J Nonlinear Optimization

### Verified

Primary MIT evidence confirms:

- Spring 2025;
- graduate level;
- linear algebra plus real analysis prerequisites;
- convex and nonconvex optimisation;
- constrained and unconstrained methods;
- first-order, second-order and geometry-aware algorithms;
- Lagrange multipliers, convex analysis and duality;
- six problem sets with solutions;
- exam and review material;
- downloadable OCW package.

Access is **F2**, not F1: OCW provides substantial learning and self-assessment material but no tracked free completion route or credential is claimed.

### Decision

**advance**

The high prerequisite burden affects recommendation breadth, not validity. This is exactly the kind of serious challenger the existing broad-optimisation gap was intended to surface.

## MIT 12.340x Global Warming Science

### Verified

MIT describes a full undergraduate climate-science course covering:

- climate energy balance;
- greenhouse gases;
- atmospheric/oceanic convection and circulation;
- solar/orbital influences;
- aerosols;
- evidence for past and present climate change;
- climate models;
- problem sets and exams.

MIT also states that the Open Learning Library route is free and that materials can be viewed without enrolment.

### Currentness caveat

The course was taught in **Spring 2020**.

That is old enough to matter in climate science: a canonical 2026 climate anchor should not silently omit newer assessment evidence or treat a six-year-old empirical snapshot as current simply because the physical foundations remain strong.

### Decision

**advance**

The course clears Shallow because it is complete, deep and directly relevant. Deep Review must explicitly test whether its age is compatible with a current best-in-class recommendation and compare the stable physical-science value against missing post-2020 developments.

## Microsoft AI Red Teaming 101

### Verified instructional core

Microsoft exposes ten modular episodes covering:

1. AI-red-team fundamentals;
2. how generative models work;
3. direct prompt injection;
4. indirect prompt injection;
5. single-turn attacks;
6. multi-turn attacks;
7. defenses;
8. PyRIT automation;
9. automated single-turn testing;
10. automated multi-turn testing.

The series includes slides, demonstrations, case studies and links to the official lab repository.

### Access correction

Discovery initially marked the apparent route F1 because Microsoft advertises hands-on labs.

The official lab repository resolves the ambiguity:

- challenges and notebooks are open;
- the playground can be self-hosted;
- actually running it requires either Azure OpenAI or standard OpenAI API credentials.

That paid/external API dependency means the complete practical experience is **not genuinely free** under the project's definition.

The correct current classification is therefore **F2_CONTENT_ONLY**.

### Decision

**advance**

The free instructional core is still far more substantial and specialised than the earlier two-hour AI-security fundamentals candidate. Deep Review should score the practice-access friction explicitly and compare whether ten modular episodes form a strong enough course-like pathway to justify a specialist canonical slot.
