## Validation-driven cleanup

The first CI pass correctly identified six discovery overlaps already present in earlier batches: Yale HIST 234, Yale HIST 119, Yale PHIL 181, MIT 6.042J, MIT 18.01SC and MIT 18.03SC. Duplicate protection was not weakened.

Those six slots were replaced with **Yale HIST 251**, **Yale HIST 276**, **MIT 24.00 Problems of Philosophy**, **MIT 18.100A Real Analysis**, **MIT 18.04 Complex Variables with Applications**, and **MIT 18.781 Theory of Numbers**. This preserves the 40-candidate target while increasing actual marginal coverage.

## Evidence caveats

1. Archive quality does not cancel age. Yale history and MIT philosophy courses remain candidates because the core scholarship can be durable, but deep review must inspect dated framing, bibliography and empirical claims.
2. FUN MOOC courses can be cohort/time-window dependent. Enrolment status and credential conditions must be rechecked immediately before publication.
3. *Les hiéroglyphes égyptiens* has free learning access but an optional paid verified certificate, so it remains F1 rather than F0 at discovery.
4. Berkeley public course sites expose substantial material, but some recordings, autograders or student services may require institutional access. Both CS 61A and CS 61C remain F2 pending shallow verification of the public route.
5. MSE's Academy of Money is excellent practical personal finance but includes UK-specific tax, mortgage and pension information; jurisdiction-sensitive currency will matter heavily in scoring.
6. Religious-studies candidates are included as academic historical/literary study, not as devotional or confessional instruction.
7. Provider concentration remains visible in Yale, MIT, OpenLearn and Saylor. The final list must be determined by head-to-head merit rather than provider volume.

Run the second saturation audit and make shallow screening the primary workstream. Any next discovery batch should be smaller and targeted rather than another uniform 40-course harvest.
