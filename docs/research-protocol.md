# Research Protocol

## Objective

Build a candidate universe broad enough that the final Top 100 is the result of comparison, not memory or brand familiarity.

## Phase 1 — Discovery

Target **300–500 candidates** across all categories. Sources include major universities, open-course initiatives, public institutions, specialist academies and high-quality industry training.

Discovery is intentionally permissive. Inclusion in the candidate pool is not endorsement.

## Phase 2 — Shallow screen

Reject obvious failures:

- F3-only access;
- dead/abandoned course with no usable archive;
- poor instructional completeness;
- duplicated mirror when an official source exists;
- insufficient evidence;
- narrow marketing material presented as education.

## Phase 3 — Deep review

For finalists, verify:

1. syllabus and scope;
2. teaching quality;
3. exercises/projects/labs;
4. prerequisites;
5. currency relative to subject;
6. instructor/provider expertise;
7. access model and hidden paywalls;
8. certificate/credit claims;
9. instruction language and subtitle languages;
10. strongest direct alternatives.

## Phase 4 — Head-to-head admission

A new course does not enter simply because it is good. It should beat or materially complement the weakest incumbent in its category. This prevents list inflation.

## Phase 5 — Publish

Publish global and category rankings only after the dataset reaches coverage saturation: repeated discovery passes across every category should stop producing plausible A+/S candidates at a meaningful rate.

## Research log

Every deep review should record the date and evidence URLs. Important score changes should be explainable from a commit or review note.
