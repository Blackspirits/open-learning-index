**Audit date:** 2026-09-12

**Reference fixtures:** 17

## Executive conclusion

The discovery universe is **saturated enough to stop broad discovery**.

All 19 categories now have at least five active survivors, and the project has **207 advance/hold candidates** for an eventual public index of roughly **100–150 resources**. The main quality problem is no longer candidate volume; it is deep comparison, provider concentration, English-language concentration and aggressive finalist pruning.

The earlier 320–350 candidate target is therefore superseded by evidence from completed screening. Expanding from 286 merely to satisfy the old numerical target would reduce signal-to-noise and create unnecessary deep-review debt.

A **small corrective discovery pass is justified**, but only for two demonstrated structural gaps:

1. a complete, genuinely free **Arabic** learning pathway;
2. a complete, genuinely free **Mandarin Chinese** learning pathway.

A secondary, lower-priority search may admit a very small number of exceptional **non-Western History/Humanities challengers** if they clearly compete with the existing finalists rather than merely increase geographic diversity.

This is a micro-discovery gate, not another general harvesting wave.

## Why broad discovery stops here

### 1. The survivor pool already exceeds the intended publication size

The current survivor pool contains **207 candidates**. The project intends to publish only about 100–150 exceptional resources. At least 57–107 additional eliminations are therefore expected during deep review and head-to-head admission even if neither remaining hold is rejected.

Adding another 30–60 discovery records would increase review cost without evidence that the final index lacks competitive candidates.

### 2. Screening has already demonstrated real selectivity

The project rejected **79 of 286 candidates** before final scoring. Rejections include prestigious providers where material was stale, incomplete, too narrow, redundant or dependent on inaccessible infrastructure. This confirms that the pool is no longer a loose discovery list; it is a credible deep-review universe.

### 3. Every category has multiple plausible competitors

The purpose of discovery is to create enough competition that weak or redundant candidates can be eliminated. That condition is now met across all categories.

Additional discovery is warranted only where the *type* of coverage is missing, not where the raw count feels low.

## Structural weaknesses that remain

### English-language concentration

Current screening metadata records English instruction for **194 of 207 active survivors**. Some providers expose translations or alternate-language variants that are not fully represented in this count, so it must not be interpreted as a perfect language census. The signal is nevertheless unambiguous: the active pool is heavily Anglophone.

This should influence deep-review recommendation diversity and future monthly discovery, but it does **not** justify a large immediate multilingual harvesting wave. Original-language candidates still need to beat the same quality bar.

### Provider concentration

The four largest survivor providers are:

- Saylor University — 24;
- MIT OpenCourseWare — 22;
- OpenLearn — 21;
- Open Yale Courses — 16.

Together they account for **83 of 207 survivors (~40%)**.

This is a deep-review warning, not an automatic defect. Strong providers may legitimately place multiple courses, but the public index should not become a catalogue mirror. Head-to-head admission must explicitly penalise redundant provider clusters where equivalent or better alternatives exist.

### History/Humanities geographic concentration

History & Culture and Humanities & Philosophy are numerically rich — 16 and 15 survivors respectively — but the pool still leans heavily toward Western/Anglophone institutions and canonical material.

Do not reopen broad history/humanities discovery. A new candidate should enter only if it is an unusually strong course centred on an underrepresented region/tradition and plausibly competitive with current survivors.

### Two evidence-constrained holds

The only current holds are:

- European School Education Platform — *Developing students' AI literacy*: strong 2026 course, but zero-cost access for this exact course remains insufficiently explicit;
- NAU — *Consumidor Ciberseguro*: active pt-PT course, but a reliable update date is not exposed for a legal/cybersecurity topic where currency is material.

Neither hold blocks the final audit. They can remain explicit unresolved records until new evidence appears.

## Languages: the only clear discovery gap

The current Languages survivor set contains complete or substantial pathways for:

- German — Deutsche Welle *Nicos Weg* A1–B1;
- Spanish — Language Transfer *Complete Spanish*;
- French — TV5MONDE *Première classe*;
- Japanese — Japan Foundation Minato *Marugoto A1-1*;
- Greek — Language Transfer *Complete Greek*;
- Swahili — Language Transfer *Complete Swahili*;
- English — freeCodeCamp A2 and B1 English for Developers;
- language-learning strategy — OpenLearn *How to learn a language*.

The candidate pool also researched Arabic and Mandarin Chinese, but both failed the completeness bar:

- Language Transfer *Intro to Arabic* was rejected as introductory with unresolved variety coverage;
- freeCodeCamp *A1 Professional Chinese* was rejected while still a public beta/incomplete path.

This is a genuine coverage gap rather than a numeric gap. Arabic and Mandarin are major world languages, and the index currently has no complete finalist-level pathway for either.

## Final targeted discovery decision

Approve **one micro-discovery pass of at most 8–12 candidates**, with permission to stop early.

### Priority A — required search

Search for serious challengers for:

1. **Arabic** — preferably a coherent beginner-to-substantial pathway with explicit variety/register, practice and a fully free learner route;
2. **Mandarin Chinese** — preferably a coherent beginner pathway with listening, speaking, reading/characters and assessment/practice.

Aim for **2–4 credible candidates per language**, not a quota. If only one candidate clears the discovery bar, record one. If none do, retain the gap rather than lowering standards.

### Priority B — optional search

Up to **2–4 total** History/Humanities candidates may be added only if they:

- centre an underrepresented African, Asian, Latin American, Middle Eastern or other non-Western region/tradition;
- are complete and genuinely free;
- are pedagogically strong enough to challenge a current survivor;
- improve the final index rather than merely diversify metadata.

### Explicitly out of scope

Do not run another general search across:

- AI/data or computer science;
- mathematics/sciences/engineering;
- marketing/business/finance;
- health/medicine;
- cybersecurity;
- arts/design;
- general law/public policy;
- education;
- writing/communication.

These areas already have sufficient candidate competition for deep review.

## Stop conditions for the micro-discovery pass

Stop immediately when any of the following becomes true:

1. Arabic and Mandarin each have at least one serious complete challenger in the pool;
2. additional search results are partial, paid, obsolete or materially weaker than existing survivors;
3. the wave reaches **12 new candidates**;
4. marginal discoveries no longer plausibly compete for the eventual public index.

Every added candidate must proceed directly through shallow screening. Do not accumulate another unscreened backlog.

## What happens after the micro-discovery pass

1. shallow-screen any newly added candidates immediately;
2. freeze v0.2 discovery again;
3. begin deep review by category clusters;
4. compare survivors head-to-head rather than score them in isolation;
5. reduce the current ~207-survivor pool toward the intended 100–150 publication set;
6. preserve the two unresolved holds until their evidence changes or a later gate explicitly rejects them.

## Final decision

**Broad discovery: STOP.**  
**Targeted discovery: YES, one micro-wave capped at 8–12 candidates, focused primarily on complete Arabic and Mandarin pathways.**  
**Deep review: next primary workstream immediately after that micro-wave.**

The old numerical goal of 320–350 candidates is retired. Saturation is now determined by competitive coverage and marginal value, not by reaching a round candidate count.
