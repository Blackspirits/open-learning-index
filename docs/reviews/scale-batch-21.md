## Main findings

### Herodotus

This is the strongest short methodological addition in the batch.

The six-hour course was first published and updated in April 2024. It uses Herodotus not merely as ancient-history content but as a structured exercise in historical enquiry: learners analyse passages, compare accounts, assess reliability and reflect on how historical claims are built.

Its Depth score is deliberately constrained by duration. Its high recommendation comes from distinctiveness and transferability rather than breadth.

### Female protest and suffrage

The ten-hour 2021 course is narrower than a general British-history survey but unusually strong for independent learning.

It explicitly teaches learners to use primary and secondary sources, build chronology, interpret media and distinguish description, explanation and analysis. The Ada Nield Chew and May Billinghurst case studies give the course a concrete source-based structure rather than a purely narrative one.

### Modern Revolutions

HIST362 is the strongest current long-form specialist course in this batch.

The current syllabus estimates 39 hours, includes end-of-unit assessments, an unlimited practice exam, a graded final exam, a free completion certificate and 3.9 CEUs. The syllabus was last modified on **3 October 2025**.

Its comparative scope is materially different from Yale HIST 116: it treats revolutionary causes, ideologies, violence, social groups and consequences across multiple regions rather than maximising depth on a single national revolution.

### Yale HIST 116

Joanne Freeman's 25-lecture course remains excellent on historical contingency, political culture, identity and the process by which colonists became revolutionaries and then Americans.

The public archive preserves video, audio, transcripts, syllabus and readings. The original course also required two papers, a midterm and final.

The independent route cannot reproduce that feedback/assessment environment, and the 2010 bibliography predates substantial later scholarship. Those are real Phase 3 limits even though the core teaching remains strong.

### The Enlightenment

This remains a useful 16-hour Level 2 OpenLearn course connecting Enlightenment thought across science, religion, art, nature, the classics and the transition to Romanticism.

The last substantive update is May 2019. In a stable historical subject that is not a blocker, but the older frame gives less attention to newer global/colonial historiography than a current course should. The score therefore remains below newer OpenLearn history material.
