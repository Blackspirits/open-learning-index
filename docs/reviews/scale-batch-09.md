# Deep Review scale batch 09 — Law & Public Policy

## Purpose

Complete Phase 3 Deep Review for the current Law & Public Policy `advance` pool after correcting a legal-currentness false positive.

Law is treated as a currency-sensitive category. Active enrolment, a recent cohort launch or institutional prestige do **not** by themselves prove that legal content, case law or regulatory references are current.

## Pre-review maintenance gate

Before scoring this batch, `coe-help-protection-safety-journalists` was moved from `advance` to `hold` in #45.

The April 2026 Albanian launch confirms that the course remains active, but the Council of Europe generic self-learning description still identifies the underlying course as published in July 2020. No substantive 2025/2026 revision to the generic legal content was verified.

Because the course covers evolving legal standards, protection mechanisms and case-law-sensitive material, an active 2026 launch was not accepted as a substitute for content-currentness evidence.

## Council of Europe HELP family

### Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights

`coe-help-ai-human-rights` is one of the clearest current legal-tech recommendations in the index.

The Council of Europe states that the course was initially developed in 2024 and comprehensively updated in 2025. The revision explicitly incorporates:

- the Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law;
- HUDERIA risk and impact assessment;
- privacy and personal-data protection;
- non-discrimination;
- freedom of expression;
- AI and justice;
- broader European and international legal frameworks.

The learning design also has unusually concrete public evidence: presentations, interactive screens, knowledge tests and reflective exercises.

Its limit is scope, not quality. Six hours cannot replace a full AI-law curriculum.

### Cybercrime and Electronic Evidence

`coe-help-cybercrime-electronic-evidence` covers seven substantive modules and roughly ten hours:

1. computers, networks and cybercrime;
2. Budapest Convention foundations;
3. substantive provisions;
4. electronic evidence and procedural powers;
5. international cooperation;
6. cybercrime in practice and human rights;
7. the Second Additional Protocol.

The Council of Europe explicitly identified Cybercrime as an **updated course in 2026**.

However, the detailed public module descriptions remain largely attached to the earlier published version. The review therefore gives strong Currency but does not pretend that the exact 2026 revision delta or all current assessment mechanics are publicly visible.

### Data Protection and Privacy Rights

`coe-help-data-protection-privacy-rights` is the strongest broad European privacy-law candidate in the current pool.

The course covers:

- CoE and EU legal frameworks;
- GDPR and modernised Convention 108+;
- ECtHR and CJEU case law;
- health data;
- media;
- e-communications;
- new technologies;
- workplace privacy;
- data-subject rights;
- enforcement and international data flows.

The Council of Europe explicitly presented it as an **updated HELP course in 2026**.

Older public descriptions document interactive exercises and multiple-choice learning checks. Because the newest revision is not publicly diffed in detail, the Practice score is deliberately conservative rather than assuming every old mechanic persists unchanged.

## WIPO head-to-head

### DL-101 vs IP Panorama 2.0

These courses are complementary rather than redundant.

`wipo-dl101-intellectual-property` remains the stronger general IP foundation:

- 55 hours;
- broad international IP coverage;
- module tests and final assessment;
- free certificate;
- **8.66 Quality / 8.8 Recommendation**.

`wipo-ip-panorama-2` serves a different learner:

- 20 hours;
- eight modules;
- interactive micro-learning;
- real-world cases;
- IP strategy, audit, infringement and commercial utilisation;
- free open-access 2026 session;
- no certificate or formal scored assessment verified.

DL-101 should generally win for legal/IP foundations. IP Panorama is the stronger lower-friction option for entrepreneurs and business learners who want applied IP strategy.

## Portuguese-language coverage

`nau-direitos-deveres-digital` fills a genuine accessibility gap.

Official Portuguese government evidence identifies it among nine new Academia Portugal Digital courses launched in May 2025. The current NAU route is four hours, bilingual in Portuguese and English, and contains twelve modules with compulsory module checks plus a final assessment.

The course is intentionally not scored as if it were specialist legal education.

Its main value is:

- low-friction digital-rights awareness;
- EU digital-rights context;
- privacy and freedom-of-expression literacy;
- online ethics;
- cyberbullying, hate speech, fraud and cybercrime awareness;
- citizenship and media literacy.

Depth is therefore the lowest in the batch, while Accessibility is the highest.

The access tier remains **F1_FULL_ASSESSMENTS**. A government launch describes the new courses as certified, but the current course page does not expose sufficiently precise course-specific credential mechanics to override the existing conservative screening record.

## Legal-currentness rules reinforced

This batch establishes several useful rules for Phase 3 and future monitoring:

1. **Recent launch ≠ recent content revision.**
   - The journalist-safety HELP course was moved to hold because a 2026 cohort launch did not establish that the generic 2020 legal content had been revised.

2. **Explicit provider update can resolve a legal-currentness blocker.**
   - Cybercrime and Data Protection were reported by the Council of Europe as updated in 2026.

3. **Revision transparency still affects scoring.**
   - Where the provider confirms an update but does not publish a detailed change log or current assessment structure, Currency can be high while Practice/Materials remain conservative.

4. **Jurisdiction matters.**
   - Saylor BUS205 remains useful but US-specific.
   - CoE HELP courses are European/international.
   - WIPO provides international IP framing.
   - NAU provides unusually useful pt-PT/EU digital-citizenship access.

5. **Credentials do not determine Quality.**
   - IP Panorama remains competitive without a certificate because its teaching design and applied cases add distinct value.
