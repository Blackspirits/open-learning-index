# Deep Review scale batch 07 — Languages

## Purpose

Deep Review the complete current `advance` pool for the Languages category in one coherent comparison family.

The category is evaluated more strictly than a simple content inventory. A strong language-learning resource must show some combination of:

- coherent progression;
- meaningful active practice;
- balanced skill development or an explicitly valuable specialist role;
- learner autonomy;
- current, genuinely free access;
- assessment or feedback where claimed;
- realistic accessibility for a new learner.

A course is not rewarded merely for covering a prestigious language, issuing a certificate or containing a large quantity of media.

## Score distribution

- Quality range: **7.72–9.50**
- Quality mean: **8.83**
- Recommendation range: **7.6–9.7**
- Recommendation mean: **8.92**

The range is healthy. Shallow `advance` means "worth Deep Review", not "all finalists are near-equal".

## German — category-leader signal

`dw-nicos-weg-a1-b1` is the strongest language-specific learning pathway in this batch.

The decisive advantage is completeness:

- three CEFR levels, A1 through B1;
- a continuous narrative rather than disconnected phrase lists;
- video and audio;
- vocabulary and grammar work;
- large quantities of interactive practice;
- tests and continuing official availability.

Its original production is not new, but Deutsche Welle still actively surfaces Nicos Weg in current learning and exam-preparation materials. German fundamentals and everyday scenarios have not aged enough to justify a strong currency penalty.

At **9.50 Quality / 9.7 Recommendation**, it is a category-leader candidate for later Phase 4, not an automatic final admission.

## Japanese — Minato / Marugoto

`minato-marugoto-a1-1-integrated` is the strongest beginner Japanese candidate in the current pool.

The official current course documents:

- **48 hours** expected study;
- registration **any time**;
- free access;
- four-skill learning;
- explicit communicative Can-do outcomes;
- study progress, assignments and tests;
- a 60-point completion threshold;
- six months of access after the learner starts.

Its main limitation is scope: it is A1-1, not a single route through multiple CEFR-equivalent levels.

Its **9.23 / 9.5** score reflects exceptional beginner-course design without pretending that one A1 stage is a complete Japanese curriculum.

## French — TV5MONDE Première classe

`tv5monde-premiere-classe-french` remains an unusually strong A1 route.

TV5MONDE currently directs true beginners to the pathway and documents more than **500 exercises** around practical needs such as introductions, housing, shopping, work, health and travel.

The course combines:

- authentic video;
- subtitles and dynamic transcripts;
- multilingual support;
- autocorrection;
- progress tracking;
- vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation and intercultural learning.

Its 2022 refresh is sufficiently current for stable A1 French.

At **9.10 / 9.3**, it is a strong category finalist, while its A1-only scope prevents it from matching Nicos Weg's multi-level completeness.

## Language Transfer — exceptional method, deliberate scope penalty

The three complete Language Transfer courses are evaluated as specialist structural foundations, not as full four-skill curricula.

### Complete Spanish

`language-transfer-complete-spanish` uses **90 tracks** and persistent prompted production.

Its strongest feature is the Thinking Method: learners derive and produce structures before hearing the answer, turning an audio course into active cognitive practice.

It scores **8.79 / 9.1** because it is unusually effective for grammar and sentence-building, but it needs external:

- vocabulary expansion;
- reading;
- writing;
- native-speaker listening;
- communicative interaction.

### Complete Greek

`language-transfer-complete-greek` extends the same approach across **120 tracks** and offers exceptional free structural depth in a language with fewer high-quality open alternatives.

Score: **8.80 / 9.0**.

### Complete Swahili

`language-transfer-complete-swahili` provides **110 tracks**, an especially valuable contribution to geographic/language diversity.

Score: **8.70 / 8.9**.

All Language Transfer material remains genuinely free; donations do not unlock additional learning content.

The absence of a certificate is not penalised as a quality defect. The absence of integrated assessment and balanced four-skill practice is.

## English for Developers

The two freeCodeCamp pathways are now real completion routes rather than merely experimental previews.

### A2 English for Developers

`freecodecamp-a2-english-developers` combines workplace/developer scenarios with audio, dialogue, fill-in-the-blank work, explanation and a live certification exam.

Score: **8.89 / 8.9**.

### B1 English for Developers

`freecodecamp-b1-english-developers` advances into feedback, collaboration, decisions, projects and more complex professional functions.

Score: **8.96 / 9.0**.

The certification exams became fully live in December 2025. The public curriculum still carries a Beta label, so the review preserves a modest maturity caveat rather than treating the certification launch as proof of finished product quality.

The courses are especially recommendable to developers and less appropriate as general English pathways.

## Mandarin — BLCU vs MandarinX

This is the clearest head-to-head outcome in the batch.

### BLCU / UMOOCs

`blcu-umoocs-elementary-spoken-chinese` is currently in its fifteenth run:

- **24 August–27 December 2026**;
- 18 weeks;
- 2–3 hours recommended weekly;
- 32 videos;
- 10 assessments;
- 32 downloadable resources;
- tests/exams/discussion contribute to the final grade;
- 60 points is the stated passing threshold.

The course is built by Beijing Language and Culture University and uses task-oriented spoken Mandarin.

Its **8.82 Quality** is strong.

Recommendation falls to **8.5** because the public learner environment and instruction-language support are not clearly designed for an international zero-beginner who cannot navigate Chinese.

### MandarinX Level 1

`mandarinx-mandarin-chinese-level-1` remains a legitimate F2 candidate because MandarinX explicitly states that its edX courses can be audited for free.

However, the free route is weaker in the comparison:

- roughly six weeks;
- less free assessment evidence;
- verified certificate is paid;
- some pronunciation/enrichment features are associated with the paid track.

It scores **7.72 / 7.6**.

That is not a rejection at Phase 3. It is a clear signal that BLCU currently dominates the free Mandarin comparison where accessibility permits.

## Arabic — broad pathway vs specialist grammar

### Al Jazeera Learning Arabic

`aljazeera-learning-arabic-general-language` is the stronger all-round Arabic candidate.

The provider explicitly describes the site as a **free open educational source**. The General Language ecosystem spans introductory through proficiency material and includes:

- Arabic letters and sounds;
- everyday communication;
- grammar;
- authentic and literary texts;
- audio;
- show/hide text and vocalisation;
- exercise results;
- placement testing;
- dictionary/vocalisation tools.

Its main weakness is not content quantity but structure: this is a non-linear open learning environment rather than a fixed course with one completion path.

Score: **8.88 / 9.0**.

### Madinah Arabic Grammar Course

`madinah-arabic-grammar-course` is much more specialised.

The free sequence exposes **90+ grammar and morphology lessons**, audio-supported examples and interactive exercises. Learners who cannot read the script are sent to a separate free Arabic Reading Course first.

Score: **8.47 / 8.4**.

It is highly valuable for grammar depth but should not displace Al Jazeera as the more complete Arabic learning ecosystem.

## Learning how to learn

`openlearn-how-learn-language` occupies a deliberately separate role.

Its 24-hour, eight-week structure teaches learners to:

- set realistic goals;
- sustain motivation;
- build vocabulary;
- practise speaking, listening, reading and writing;
- evaluate resources;
- construct virtual immersion;
- establish routines and support networks.

Weekly quizzes and two compulsory badge quizzes provide assessment, and the Statement of Participation/digital badge remain free.

The formal update date is 2020, but most learning-strategy content is stable. Digital-tool examples are the main currency risk.

At **8.96 / 9.0**, it is a strong complement to practically every target-language route, not a competitor that teaches a target language itself.

## Access-model lessons

This category demonstrates why the access taxonomy must stay separate from Quality.

- Language Transfer is F2 yet pedagogically exceptional.
- freeCodeCamp and OpenLearn offer free credentials, but those credentials do not automatically raise teaching scores.
- MandarinX's free audit remains a legitimate F2 route while paid verified-track extras are excluded from scoring.
- BLCU is F1 and highly assessed without a verified free credential.
- TV5MONDE and Nicos Weg are valuable because of practice and pathway design, not because of certificates.

## Monitoring risks

The main future checks are:

1. **BLCU** — current session closes **27 December 2026**; verify a new free run before treating access as continuous.
2. **Minato** — registration is currently anytime, but each learner receives a six-month attendance window after starting.
3. **freeCodeCamp A2/B1** — continue monitoring the Beta label, exam mechanics and curriculum changes.
4. **MandarinX / edX** — re-check that free audit still exposes the substantive core and that more learning elements do not migrate behind verified access.
5. **Language Transfer** — monitor route availability because the project is maintained by a very small independent operation.
