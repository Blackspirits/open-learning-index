## Workplace communication

### OpenLearn — Effective communication in the workplace

This is the strongest general independent recommendation in the category.

The route provides:

- 24 hours over eight weeks;
- verbal, non-verbal and written communication;
- listening and self-awareness;
- meetings, presentations and networking;
- email and report writing;
- difficult and diverse situations;
- weekly quizzes;
- badge quizzes in Weeks 4 and 8;
- a recurring personal communication-development plan;
- free Open University badge and Statement of Participation;
- 24 CPD points.

The course was last formally updated on **7 April 2020**.

That produces a real but contained currency penalty. Core listening, writing and interpersonal communication skills remain sound, while some technology/future-of-work examples — including Skype-era references — are visibly dated.

### MIT 15.279 — Management Communication for Undergraduates

MIT 15.279 has the strongest original practice architecture in the current pool.

The Fall 2012 course originally included:

- baseline and final self-evaluation memos;
- concise email and persuasive-writing exercises;
- case memo;
- cover letter;
- team report;
- Q&A presentation;
- interactive presentation;
- team presentation;
- presentation recordings and self-review;
- peer/instructor feedback;
- teamwork exercises;
- rubrics for professional writing and oral presentation.

OCW also exposes material on active listening, difficult conversations, intercultural communication, media relations, persuasion, tables/graphs and writing style.

This justifies **9.08 Quality**.

However, an independent OCW learner cannot reproduce much of the original feedback, team and live-presentation environment. The 2012 course also predates modern remote/hybrid work and generative-AI communication contexts.

Recommendation therefore drops to **8.5**.

This is an important Phase 3 distinction: excellent original pedagogy is not identical to excellent current independent-study usability.

## Writing

### OpenLearn — Start writing fiction

The candidate URL points to the **12-hour** `Start writing fiction` course and is reviewed as such; it is not silently replaced by a different 24-hour OpenLearn fiction course.

The route develops:

- character;
- setting;
- genre;
- observation;
- drafting;
- reflection on strengths and weaknesses;
- practical fiction exercises.

It was formally updated **14 May 2026**.

Practice is central, but the route lacks external workshop critique. That keeps it below a fully feedback-rich creative-writing programme while still making it the strongest current fiction starter in the pool.

### OpenLearn — Essay and report writing skills

This 15-hour route follows the actual academic-writing process:

- interpreting assignment tasks;
- distinguishing essays from reports;
- researching and evaluating sources;
- brainstorming and mind maps;
- planning;
- drafting;
- introductions, bodies and conclusions;
- polishing;
- learning from feedback.

It was last updated in **April 2019**.

Academic-writing fundamentals are stable, so age is a moderate rather than severe weakness. The larger limitation is that the course cannot provide personalised feedback on the learner's own essays.

## Crisis communication

### Saylor PRDV107

The current 20-hour specialist route covers:

1. crisis communication foundations;
2. preparation;
3. crisis-management teams;
4. social media during a crisis;
5. crisis communication plans;
6. strategic message development.

The current syllabus was modified **17 September 2025**.

This recency matters more here than for generic writing because social-media channels and crisis-response expectations change materially.

Assessment includes unit checks, practice exam and final exam. The route is useful for principles and planning, but real organisational crisis work still requires context-specific protocols, legal review and exercises.

## Currency rules reinforced

1. **Writing fundamentals are relatively stable.**
   - A 2019 academic-writing course can remain useful when the core workflow is sound.

2. **Communication technology ages faster than writing process.**
   - OpenLearn's 2020 workplace examples receive a moderate currentness penalty.

3. **Original classroom practice may not survive open archival delivery.**
   - MIT 15.279 receives very high Practice for its original design but lower Accessibility/Recommendation for current independent learners.

4. **Currentness matters strongly in crisis communication.**
   - Social-media/platform practice and response norms can change quickly.

5. **Certificates do not replace authentic production.**
   - Auto-graded final exams help, but writing/presentation feedback remains a major differentiator.
