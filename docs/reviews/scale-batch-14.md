## MIT 6.01SC

This course is one of the clearest examples in the index of high intrinsic learning quality but reduced current recommendation.

The original course integrates:

- software engineering;
- circuits;
- state machines;
- signals and systems;
- control;
- probability;
- Bayesian reasoning;
- robotics;
- software labs;
- design labs;
- homework;
- nano-quizzes;
- midterms and final exam.

MIT OCW also provides lecture/recitation videos, an open textbook/course notes, assignments and exam material.

However, the original lab environment now creates real friction:

- the course used the legacy `lib601` software stack;
- some labs relied on ActivMedia Pioneer 3-DX robots;
- the original online tutorial system is not available in OCW;
- some design-lab elements cannot be reproduced without obsolete hardware.

The durable systems, circuits and control concepts remain valuable. The course therefore retains **8.98 Quality**, but Recommendation drops to **7.9**.

## Nordic Bluetooth Low Energy Fundamentals

This is a strong current embedded-wireless course:

- 6 lessons;
- 8–10 hours;
- quizzes in every lesson;
- many build/flash/debug exercises;
- GAP, ATT/GATT and PHY;
- advertising;
- connections;
- services and characteristics;
- security;
- Bluetooth packet capture/sniffing;
- free completion certificate.

The current course supports nRF Connect SDK versions through **v3.4.0** and current nRF54/nRF53/nRF52 development kits.

Its main constraint is accessibility, not pedagogy.

The complete route requires:

- compatible Nordic development hardware;
- a smartphone/tablet;
- installed Nordic tooling;
- extra hardware for parts of the packet-sniffing lesson.

This mirrors the pattern already seen in the other Nordic reviews: very high Quality but more moderate Recommendation because the learning route is tightly coupled to one hardware/vendor ecosystem.

## MathWorks Onramps

MathWorks currently states that all Onramp courses are:

- free for everyone;
- available without an Online Training Suite subscription;
- usable online without owning the product licence;
- non-expiring.

All three reviewed Onramps provide browser-based practical work, automated feedback and free completion recognition.

### Simulink Onramp

The strongest of the three for engineering learning.

It includes:

- blocks and signals;
- simulation;
- signal inspection;
- math/logic algorithms;
- MATLAB integration;
- discrete and continuous dynamic systems;
- automotive performance-mode project;
- thermostat project;
- peregrine-falcon dive project.

The course's project structure is unusually good for a free vendor Onramp.

Its **9.0 Recommendation** is still below what a vendor-neutral model-based systems curriculum could earn because the skills are strongly tied to Simulink.

### MATLAB Onramp

The strongest general introduction to MATLAB in the pool.

It covers:

- commands and variables;
- scripts and debugging;
- arrays and indexing;
- functions;
- plots;
- data import;
- logical arrays;
- control flow;
- a final project using stellar motion/spectra.

Automated browser feedback makes the route unusually efficient.

Its main limitation is product transferability, not learning quality.

### Signal Processing Onramp

A strong practical workflow course covering:

- signal import;
- preprocessing;
- resampling/alignment;
- spectral analysis;
- time-frequency analysis;
- low-pass/band-pass filtering;
- measurements;
- generated MATLAB code.

It is deliberately not scored as a full DSP course because theoretical depth is limited.
