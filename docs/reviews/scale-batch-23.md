## Optimisation — MIT 6.7220J vs MathWorks Onramp

This is a decisive comparison.

MathWorks Optimization Onramp remains excellent at what it does:

- roughly two hours;
- browser execution;
- automated feedback;
- low-friction practical optimisation workflow.

Its Deep Review score is **8.65 / 8.4**.

MIT 6.7220J serves a different and much deeper role:

- full Spring 2025 graduate course;
- convex and nonconvex theory;
- constrained and unconstrained optimisation;
- optimality conditions;
- gradient, subgradient, Newton and geometry-aware methods;
- Lagrange multipliers and duality;
- six problem sets with solutions;
- examination material;
- current machine-learning/control/resource-allocation applications.

The trade-off is access: MIT expects linear algebra plus real analysis.

At **9.46 Quality / 9.2 Recommendation**, it is clearly strong enough for Phase 4 consideration. Provider concentration must be handled there, not by distorting the score here.

## Climate science — depth finally exists, currentness still hurts

The earlier climate alternatives did not solve the Natural Sciences gap:

- FAO climate-smart agriculture: **7.92 / 8.0**, authoritative but approximately 90 minutes;
- EU Climate Action Academy: **8.53 / 8.5**, exceptionally current but centred on action, communication and policy rather than disciplinary climate science.

MIT 12.340x reverses that problem.

It provides real climate-science depth:

- radiative balance and greenhouse physics;
- atmospheric and oceanic processes;
- orbital/solar variability;
- aerosols;
- evidence of climate change;
- climate modelling;
- quantitative problem sets and exams.

Intrinsic Quality is therefore **9.25**.

But the course was taught in **Spring 2020**. For stable physics this would be a modest issue; for a course intended to anchor current climate science in 2026, missing the later IPCC AR6 cycle and post-2020 observational context is significant.

Recommendation is therefore only **8.8**.

Phase 4 should decide whether this is good enough as a caveated foundations route or whether the gap should remain open for a newer full curriculum.

## Sales — the gap now has a serious challenger

HubSpot Inbound Sales scored **8.36 / 8.6** and was correctly kept out of the canonical set because its 3h06 scope was too compact to justify admission merely for coverage.

Saylor BUS633 is materially stronger:

- 29 hours;
- ten units following the selling process;
- prospecting and qualification;
- approach, presentation and objections;
- closing and follow-up;
- selling strategies and relationship building;
- CRM, e-commerce, social and other digital-sales tools;
- repeatable unit assessments;
- repeatable practice exam;
- final exam requiring 70%;
- free completion certificate;
- syllabus modified October 2025.

Quality is **8.84** and Recommendation **9.1**.

This is the first reviewed sales candidate strong enough to make the absence of a canonical sales route a genuine Phase 4 decision rather than an accepted temporary gap.

## AI security — better, but not fully free in practice

Microsoft's earlier AI Security Fundamentals path scored **7.91 / 8.4**. It was current but about two hours and too light on practice.

AI Red Teaming 101 improves substantially:

- ten episodes;
- direct and indirect prompt injection;
- single- and multi-turn attack patterns;
- defenses;
- real cases;
- PyRIT automation;
- demonstrations;
- official challenge repository and notebooks.

Currency and expertise are near the top of the scale.

The blocker is the practical access model.

The lab repository is open, but running the full playground requires:

- Docker;
- Azure OpenAI **or** standard OpenAI API credentials.

The genuinely free route is therefore F2 rather than F1.

Practice falls to **7.4**, producing **8.62 Quality / 8.7 Recommendation**.

This is a serious specialist resource, but Phase 4 should not interpret topic uniqueness as an automatic admission.
