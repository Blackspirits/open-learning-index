## Head-to-head signals

### Introductory probability and statistics

`mit-18-05-probability-statistics` is the strongest all-round route in this family.

Its main advantage is not institutional prestige: it combines rigorous probability and inference with a free Open Learning Library version that provides interactive reading questions, problem checkers and optional progress tracking.

`cmu-oli-probability-statistics` remains a category-leader candidate because its simulations and StatTutor support are exceptional. Its Open & Free route lacks scored tests and a credential, but that does not weaken its intrinsic pedagogy.

`cmu-oli-statistical-reasoning` is a strong alternative when learners need introductory statistics with a lighter empirical probability treatment.

`khan-statistics-probability` has the strongest accessibility and repeated mastery practice, but less sustained theoretical development.

### Probability depth

`harvard-stat110-probability` is the strongest probability-specialist candidate.

Its free book, lecture sequence and strategic-practice problem sets provide more probability depth than broad introductory statistics courses. It should not replace them for learners who need inference and regression as equal priorities.

### Advanced statistics

`mit-18-650-statistics-applications` is excellent but serves a different learner.

Probability theory and linear algebra are real prerequisites. Its value lies in mathematical foundations for estimation, testing, regression, Bayesian methods, PCA and GLMs, not in beginner accessibility.

### Mathematics for computer science

`mit-6-042j-math-cs` is a category-leader candidate for discrete mathematics.

The open textbook, lectures, problem sets and exams form a coherent foundation in proofs, graphs, state machines, modular arithmetic, counting and discrete probability. Its natural relationship to `mit-6-006-algorithms` is complementary: 6.042J supplies mathematical foundations that algorithms courses assume.

### Calculus Scholar sequence

The three OCW Scholar courses demonstrate why age must be subject-sensitive.

- `mit-18-01sc-calculus` — Fall 2010
- `mit-18-02sc-multivariable-calculus` — Fall 2010
- `mit-18-03sc-differential-equations` — Fall 2011

All were explicitly designed for independent learners and include combinations of:

- lecture videos and notes;
- recitation/problem-solving videos;
- worked examples;
- practice problems;
- problem sets and solutions;
- exams and solutions.

The mathematical foundations remain stable. The only meaningful age penalty is minor compatibility friction around optional legacy Java Mathlets.

Artificially downranking these courses simply because they are old would violate the subject-sensitive currency model.

### Real analysis

`mit-18-100a-real-analysis` is a distinct advanced role rather than a duplicate of the calculus Scholar sequence.

It uses twelve written assignments, recitations, lecture material, a midterm and final assignment to train proof construction and rigorous analysis. Its lower Recommendation relative to Quality reflects prerequisite difficulty.

## Provider concentration

Six of the ten reviews in this batch are MIT courses.

This is a **Phase 4 risk**, not a reason to falsify Phase 3 scores.

MIT has multiple genuinely distinct high-quality mathematical learning experiences. The final index should still avoid provider over-concentration when a comparable non-MIT alternative serves the same learner need equally well.

Phase 4 should therefore ask:

1. Does each MIT course serve a materially distinct learning objective?
2. Is there a non-MIT alternative with similar quality and better accessibility or pedagogy?
3. Does retaining the course improve the index rather than merely increase institutional representation?
