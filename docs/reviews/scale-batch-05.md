# Deep Review scale batch 05 — Health & Psychology

## Purpose

Review a focused Health & Psychology comparison family with stricter currency and evidence requirements than stable academic subjects.

This batch deliberately separates:

- intrinsic teaching quality from current recommendation;
- short professional training from full academic depth;
- free credentials from pedagogical quality;
- authoritative provider status from actual public evidence.

Health, clinical research and psychology can age materially. Old but pedagogically excellent resources therefore keep credit for structure and practice while receiving explicit currency penalties where terminology, guidelines or evidence may have moved.

## General psychology head-to-head

### MIT 9.00SC vs Saylor PSYCH101

`mit-9-00sc-intro-psychology` has the strongest intrinsic teaching package in this comparison.

It was explicitly designed for independent study and combines lecture videos, readings, session-level self-checks, discussion material, substantial exams with solutions, additional exam practice and optional analytical writing.

Its **9.01 Quality** therefore remains justified.

However, Fall 2011 is materially old for psychology. Psychopathology terminology, clinical framing, empirical literature and some social-science context have evolved. That reduces Currency to 5.8 and keeps Recommendation at **8.6**.

`saylor-psych101-introduction-psychology` is the safer general recommendation for a new learner in 2026.

Its syllabus was modified in 2025, the route is approximately 46 hours, unit assessments are reusable, the final certificate exam is free and the completion certificate does not require payment. Its OER curation is less cohesive than MIT's single-course design, so Quality is lower at **8.57**, but Recommendation is higher at **8.9**.

The paid ACE credit pathway is explicitly separate from the free learning and certificate route.

### Complementary psychology courses

`openlearn-forensic-psychology` is not a duplicate of a general introduction. Its 24-hour investigation narrative, witness material, weekly quizzes and two badge quizzes make it one of the strongest applied-learning experiences in this batch. The 2020 update date is the main constraint.

`openlearn-psychology-around-world` serves a different purpose again. Its main value is current cross-cultural perspective and explicit treatment of the limitations of WEIRD psychology. Six hours is too short for high Depth, but the course can materially improve a broader psychology pathway.

## Professional health and research

### ICH GCP E6(R3)

`tghn-ich-gcp-e6-r3` is the strongest narrow professional recommendation in this batch.

Its final-guideline alignment, multilingual availability, lifecycle structure and assessed certificate make it highly relevant for current clinical-research practice. The main limit is depth: roughly 180 minutes cannot replace a comprehensive clinical-research curriculum.

This is a clear example of Recommendation exceeding intrinsic Quality for a sharply defined professional need.

### Research ethics

`tghn-research-ethics-v2` offers a broader ethical foundation through ten modules, quizzes and case-oriented supporting materials.

The course is adapted from WHO material and updated with TGHN, but the public page does not expose an equally precise recent revision date. It therefore receives strong but lower Currency than GCP R3.

### Data lifecycle

`tghn-data-life-cycle` is current and practically useful for researchers who need responsible data-management and sharing foundations.

The course itself states that it is still expanding and explicitly says it is not intended to train data managers or statisticians. Those limits are preserved rather than hidden by its strong specialist authorship.

### Mental-health literacy

`nau-saude-mental-bem-estar-ensino-superior` provides rare current Portuguese-language coverage with assessed modules and qualified faculty.

Its role is mental-health literacy and first-response support for higher education. It must not be described as clinical training.

## WHO evidence gates

Provider authority is not enough to justify a Deep Review score.

### Chemical Hazards Part I — scored

`whoacademy-chemical-hazards-part-1` is scored because WHO's public evidence verifies not only free/current availability and scope but also **hands-on use of a digital tool** to identify and mitigate chemical threats.

The inaccessible JavaScript-heavy Academy route still limits what can be verified about internal assessment, credential mechanics and full material structure, so Materials and Accessibility remain conservative.

### Chemical Hazards Part II — deferred

`whoacademy-chemical-hazards-part-2` remains a current shallow `advance`.

WHO verifies that Part II teaches interpretation of environmental, clinical and classification evidence during chemical-release assessment. However, public evidence still does not expose enough granular practice, assessment or feedback mechanics to assign defensible component scores.

It remains unscored rather than receiving speculative values.

### WHO Good Practices for Clinical Trial Design and Implementation — deferred

`whoacademy-good-practices-clinical-trials` also remains a current shallow `advance`.

WHO verifies that the 2026 course is free, online, self-paced, current and grounded in WHO guidance. The public launch material describes its scientific and ethical scope and target audience.

The learner-facing Academy page remains too opaque to verify meaningful assessment, feedback and practice structure. The course is therefore deliberately excluded from this batch until stronger primary evidence is available.

## Access and credential distinctions

This batch reinforces several taxonomy rules:

- F0 credentials do not automatically raise Quality.
- F2 WHO courses can still score strongly when substantive pedagogy/practice evidence is verifiable.
- OpenLearn statements and badges are useful completion recognition but are not academic credit.
- Saylor's free certificate is distinct from its optional paid ACE credit route.
- Unknown WHO credential mechanics remain recorded as unverified rather than inferred.
