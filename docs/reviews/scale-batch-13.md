# Deep Review scale batch 13 — Humanities & Philosophy

## Purpose

Complete Phase 3 Deep Review for the entire current `humanities-philosophy` `advance` pool.

The category currently contains **15 advance / 4 reject / 0 hold** candidates. None of the 15 advances had current Deep Review evidence before this batch.

Because this is a slow-changing category, archive age is handled differently from law, technology or empirical science. Older courses are not penalized merely for age when the core object of study is a stable text, philosophical problem or canonical theoretical tradition. The main risks are instead:

- representational narrowness;
- outdated contemporary examples;
- missing newer scholarship;
- practice that depended on in-person sections or instructor feedback.

## Main conclusions

### Best broad philosophy introduction

`mit-24-00-problems-philosophy` is the strongest general philosophy entry in the current pool.

The Fall 2019 course combines:

- epistemology;
- metaphysics;
- ethics;
- philosophy of mind;
- free will;
- personal identity;
- race and gender;
- meaning of life;
- quizzes;
- oral presentation;
- three short papers;
- a rewrite;
- a 2,000–2,400-word final paper.

The writing/revision structure matters more than prestige: it directly trains argument construction rather than passive lecture consumption.

### Best practical reasoning course

`saylor-phil102-critical-thinking-logic` is the strongest practical reasoning route.

The current course:

- takes about 29 hours;
- covers argument analysis, sentential logic, Venn diagrams, fallacies, scientific reasoning and strategic reasoning;
- includes repeatable unit assessments and a final exam;
- was last modified 2 October 2025;
- provides a free certificate.

Its narrower philosophical ambition is compensated by direct transferability to academic, professional and everyday reasoning.

### Political science / political philosophy

These routes serve different roles:

- `saylor-polsc101-political-science` — broadest current disciplinary survey, 111 hours, updated October 2025;
- `yale-plsc-114-political-philosophy` — deeper primary-text engagement with Plato through Tocqueville, but heavily Western-canonical;
- `fun-boite-outils-philosophie-politique` — current French-language 20-hour route with quizzes, open reflection and free badge;
- `saylor-phil103-moral-political-philosophy` — structured moral/political philosophy with current assessments;
- `openlearn-what-is-politics` — accessible conceptual introduction, but much narrower and last updated in 2019.

No single course is allowed to stand in for both political science and political philosophy.

### Ethics

`mit-24-231-ethics` remains strong despite its 2009 archive date because the core problems and theories are stable and the course includes four substantive writing assignments.

`saylor-phil203-environmental-ethics` is the better current specialist option for environmental justice and ethics, especially because its syllabus was last modified in October 2025.

### Literary studies

The Yale humanities courses remain competitive because their practice structure is substantial:

- Modern Poetry — informal writing, midterm, two papers and final;
- Theory of Literature — two 5–7 page papers and final;
- Dante — midterm, final and ~3,000-word paper.

Archive age is a minor issue here. The more material limitation is the absence of later literary scholarship and, in some cases, dependence on copyrighted primary texts not reproduced in full.

### Religious studies

Both Yale religious-studies courses are treated explicitly as academic, historical-literary routes rather than devotional instruction.

`yale-rlst-145-hebrew-bible` uses historical-critical, source, redaction, literary and canonical methods.

`yale-rlst-152-new-testament` is particularly strong on practice because learners originally completed two six-page papers and an eight-page final paper.

### Social theory

`yale-socy-151-social-theory` remains a strong foundation in Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Smith, Marx, Nietzsche, Freud, Weber and Durkheim.

Its limitation is scope: later and more globally diverse social theory must be added separately.

## Currency rules reinforced

1. **Archive age is not itself a humanities failure.**
   - Primary texts and philosophical problems can remain fully usable decades later.

2. **The relevant currency question is often representational.**
   - Does the course omit major later traditions, voices or scholarship?

3. **Writing-intensive practice matters strongly.**
   - MIT/Yale courses retain high value where papers and revision force real argument construction.

4. **Current certification does not automatically beat deeper archives.**
   - Saylor and FUN win on currentness/access.
   - MIT/Yale often win on analytical depth and extended writing.

5. **Canonical focus must be explicit.**
   - Yale Political Philosophy is strong but not a globally representative political-philosophy curriculum.
