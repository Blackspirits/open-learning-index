## Editorial principles

### High Quality is not automatic admission

This is the clearest category so far where **excellent courses are deliberately excluded**.

- MIT Ethics: strong writing and depth, but insufficient marginal role beside MIT 24.00 and Environmental Ethics.
- Dante: Quality 9.12, but a single-author specialist adds less core coverage than literary theory and broad close reading.
- Yale Political Philosophy: academically strong, but a second English archive is less valuable than a current French route once provider concentration is considered.

### Provider concentration is a real constraint

Yale supplies many of the strongest public humanities archives. Phase 4 must not turn that accident of availability into a Yale catalogue.

The admitted Yale courses therefore need distinct roles:

- literary theory;
- broad close reading;
- one academic religious-studies exemplar;
- social theory.

The second religious-studies archive, Dante and Political Philosophy are kept in Deep Review but not promoted.

### Language diversity only counts when substantive

`fun-boite-outils-philosophie-politique` is admitted because it is a genuine 20-hour French political-philosophy course with coherent modules, quizzes/reflection and a free badge. It is not admitted merely because it is in French.

### Political science is not political philosophy

- Saylor POLSC101 covers disciplinary political science at large scale.
- FUN covers political philosophy and conceptual traditions.

They complement rather than duplicate each other.

### Religious-studies representation remains limited

RLST 152 is admitted as a **specialist academic historical-literary route**, not as a proxy for world religions.

Choosing one Yale religious-studies course controls concentration, but it does not solve broader representational coverage. This is recorded as a coverage concern rather than hidden.
