## Editorial decisions

### Currentness beats prestige in entrepreneurship

MIT 15.390 is a deliberate non-admission despite **9.02 Quality**. Its twelve assignments, rubrics, templates, venture plan and pitch work remain excellent, but a 2013 entrepreneurship course is not current enough on financing, legal context, distribution and startup practice to anchor the 2026 index.

OpenLearn's March 2026 route becomes the broad foundation, while MIT 15.393 supplies a compact current practitioner layer.

### Language diversity must carry real learning value

Two lower-scoring courses are admitted for language/access reasons, but neither is token representation:

- NAU provides **30 hours**, assessment, a diploma and **1 ECTS** in European Portuguese.
- OpenClassrooms provides a coherent, current four-hour first-project path in **French**, with a cumulative quiz.

Both add learner access that the stronger English incumbents do not.

### Project management is a ladder, not a duplicate list

The canonical set now separates three useful roles:

- `pmi-kickoff` — concise authoritative English introduction;
- `saylor-bus402-project-management` — 52-hour long-form assessed foundation;
- `openclassrooms-initiez-vous-gestion-projet` — low-friction French first-project route.

### Leadership versus management

OpenLearn wins the broad leadership-development role through theory, reflection, journaling and action planning. OpenClassrooms is retained separately because it teaches the operational work of a new manager: delegation, meetings, feedback, performance, recruitment and onboarding.

HP LIFE is not admitted because currency and a credential do not compensate for microcourse depth. Saylor PRDV231 remains a useful reviewed ethics specialist but is not necessary in the core set.
