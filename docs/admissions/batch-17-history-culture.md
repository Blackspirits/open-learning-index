## Editorial principles

### One global backbone, not multiple general surveys

Khan World History clearly wins the general role.

Saylor HIST103 remains useful, but the canonical set gains more from keeping Saylor's deeper industrialisation and revolutions specialists than from duplicating the broad survey layer.

### Archives are valuable, but concentration matters

Open Yale Courses provides several excellent History archives.

Phase 4 keeps only **one** in the core:
- Ancient Greek History.

The subject is comparatively stable, the academic depth is high, and it adds a sustained classical-history role not otherwise represented.

American Revolution, Civil War and Early Middle Ages remain reviewed specialist alternatives without allowing Yale/Western archives to dominate the category.

### Current historical method matters

OpenLearn What do historians do? becomes the core methods course.

Herodotus is excellent, but its source-literacy role overlaps both the broader methods course and the retained ancient-Greek specialist.

### Post-2020 history can change the admission case

Yale Epidemics is not rejected because older history automatically expires.

It is excluded because a course about epidemic disease whose narrative stops before COVID-19 is now missing a historically transformative event directly relevant to its central theme.

### Cultural and language diversity count when quality independently clears the bar

The French FUN music-history course earns a slot because it adds a distinct cultural-history role and non-English instructional coverage without requiring a lower quality threshold.

## Coverage gaps

Two targeted opportunities remain:

- a current, high-quality history of epidemics that includes COVID-19;
- Egyptian history/hieroglyphs only if the current FUN hold can later be resolved with stable access evidence.
