# Phase 4 admission batch 08 — Engineering & Electronics

## Scope

This batch completes Phase 4 head-to-head admission for all **9 current Engineering & Electronics `advance` candidates**.

There are no pre-existing canonical Engineering & Electronics incumbents.

The family is unusually provider-concentrated:

- 5 Nordic Developer Academy courses;
- 3 MathWorks Onramps;
- 1 MIT archive.

Phase 4 therefore has to distinguish genuine competency coverage from vendor-catalogue mirroring.

## Editorial principles

### Do not mirror vendor catalogues

Nordic has five high-quality survivors. Keeping all five would make the index look comprehensive while actually over-representing one hardware/tooling ecosystem.

BLE is retained because it is the broadest, highest-scoring protocol route and teaches widely transferable concepts including GAP, ATT/GATT, services/characteristics, security and packet analysis.

The other Nordic courses remain strong specialist recommendations in Deep Review.

### Vendor training can still earn admission

MathWorks Onramps are not admitted simply because the products are popular.

They clear the bar because:

- the courses are free for everyone;
- browser execution does not require a paid product licence;
- automated feedback creates real practice;
- the three admitted routes serve different competencies:
  - MATLAB programming/data;
  - Simulink model-based systems;
  - signal-processing workflow.

The vendor coupling is still recorded as a recommendation limitation.

### High Quality can fail current reproducibility

MIT 6.01SC remains an exceptional integrated EECS curriculum in principle. In practice, major experiential elements depended on:

- legacy `lib601`;
- Pioneer 3-DX robots;
- an unavailable tutorial system;
- design-lab hardware no longer reproducible from the public route.

Phase 4 therefore does not promote it despite **8.98 Quality**.

## Vendor-neutral engineering coverage gap

This batch leaves a deliberate gap.

The canonical set still lacks a modern, deep, genuinely free, **vendor-neutral** introduction to electronics / embedded systems / integrated EECS with reproducible labs.

That is a concrete Phase 4 blind spot under the existing discovery-freeze rule and justifies a later targeted corrective discovery pass. It does not justify admitting an obsolete archive or multiple vendor courses as substitutes.
