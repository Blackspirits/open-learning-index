## Editorial principles

### Do not confuse a provider pathway with the canonical index

Cisco has an unusually strong free curriculum. That does not mean every step belongs in the index.

The admitted Cisco courses each serve a distinct role:

- Networking Basics — prerequisite networking foundation;
- Endpoint Security — endpoint defense;
- Network Defense — network controls and defensive telemetry;
- Cyber Threat Management — governance, risk and incident response;
- Ethical Hacker — broad offensive methodology.

The introductory, operating-systems and incremental device-configuration courses remain reviewed but are not promoted.

### PortSwigger remains the web-security incumbent

Cisco Ethical Hacker does not displace `portswigger-web-security-academy`.

PortSwigger remains superior for continuously updated web-application security and extensive interactive labs. Cisco adds a broader offensive-security route spanning scoping, vulnerability assessment, exploitation methodology, reporting and mitigation.

### Beginner redundancy is aggressively pruned

OpenLearn wins the general entry role because it provides 24 hours of current material and was updated in March 2026.

Cisco Introduction to Cybersecurity and IBM SkillsBuild remain useful shorter alternatives, but neither adds enough distinct coverage to justify extra core slots.

### High-change specialist gaps do not justify lowering the bar

Microsoft AI Security Fundamentals is current and relevant, but roughly two hours is too shallow for a canonical specialist slot.

AI security therefore remains a **coverage gap to monitor**. A stronger current free course could justify targeted corrective discovery; the index should not promote a placeholder merely because the topic is fashionable.
