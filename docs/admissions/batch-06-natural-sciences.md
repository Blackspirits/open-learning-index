## Head-to-head principles

### Keep complementary learning modes, not redundant credentials

General chemistry and biology each retain two courses because the pairs are pedagogically distinct:

- MIT provides deeper university-style lectures, recitations/problem sets and formal exams.
- Khan provides continuous low-friction mastery assessment, repeated checking and easier self-navigation.

Saylor's certificate-bearing alternatives remain useful reviewed resources, but a free credential is not enough to justify a third overlapping foundation.

### Stable science can age differently from empirical/frontier science

MIT Classical Mechanics (2016), MIT Chemical Science (2014) and the older Yale physics/organic-chemistry archives remain defensible because their core conceptual content is highly stable.

MIT Introductory Biology (2018) is treated differently. It is admitted for depth and academic practice, but its recommendation explicitly requires current supplementation on genomics, methods and newer biological findings. Khan is retained as the more current general biology default.

### Sequences can justify same-provider concentration

Four Yale records are admitted, but they form two coherent sequential pathways:

- Organic Chemistry I → Organic Chemistry II
- Fundamentals of Physics I → Fundamentals of Physics II

The second course in each pair adds materially different content. This is not provider-catalogue mirroring.

### Scientific practice is a distinct learning need

NASA Open Science 101 is admitted even though it is cross-disciplinary. FAIR practice, open data/code/results, repositories, version control, licensing, preregistration, research ethics and modern workflow practice are valuable scientific competencies not covered by the disciplinary courses.

Its category placement can be revisited later without weakening the admission case.

## Climate-science coverage gap

This batch deliberately **does not admit a climate-related course**.

- FAO is an approximately 90-minute climate-smart-agriculture primer.
- EU Climate Action Academy is current and useful, but focused on climate action, communication, citizen science and policy participation rather than disciplinary climate science.

Admitting either as the Natural Sciences climate anchor would hide a real coverage weakness.

This is therefore a **concrete Phase 4 blind spot** under the repository's discovery-freeze rule: a future targeted discovery pass for a deep, current, genuinely free climate-science course is justified. No broad discovery restart is implied.
